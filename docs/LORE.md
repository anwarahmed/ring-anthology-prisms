# Lore Bible

The reference for every story card, character, and level in Ring Anthology: Prisms.
**Nothing here is invented.** Every fact comes from the Halo games, novels, or official
lore, and is checked against [Halopedia](https://www.halopedia.org). Facts that haven't
been checked yet are marked **(verify)**. The `lore-check` skill confirms them before a
level uses them.

Ring Anthology: Prisms is an unofficial fan game. Halo, its characters, and its lore
belong to Microsoft. See [LEGAL.md](LEGAL.md).

## The Archive

There is no narrator character. Between levels, the Archive shows neutral timeline
cards: date, place, what happened, and the source. See "The Archive" in
[CAMPAIGN.md](CAMPAIGN.md).

## The Arbiter

The title changes meaning over the timeline, and the Archive tracks it from honor to
shame and back to honor.

1. **Before the Covenant:** a ruler and judge of the Sangheili clans (Vema 'Togad, 938 BCE).
2. **After the Writ of Union:** a position of power in the Covenant military (Jeht 'Lcmutee, 784 BCE).
3. **c. 2152:** after Fal 'Chavamee, a mark of shame for the disgraced, given for suicide missions.
4. **2462 and 2531:** Heki 'Gibadee (the Unggoy Rebellion) and Ripa 'Moramee (*Halo Wars*), the 17th in the line.
5. **2552:** Thel 'Vadamee, the last Arbiter of the Covenant era. He turns against the Prophets.
6. **After the war:** a badge of honor. Thel leads the Swords of Sanghelios.

## Kid-friendly presentation

These rules change how events are shown, never what happened.

- **Defeat:** enemies vanish in a puff of colored light. No blood, no bodies.
- **Deaths in the story** are told on Archive cards, gently, and never shown.
- **Composing** is "turning people into hard-light data". It's wrong because it takes away who they are.
- **Firing the Halos and glassing** are shown on the star map as "life went quiet" or "the surface burned". They're never seen in gameplay.
- **The Flood** are goofy-gross, never horror.
- **Every faction is presented with its canon reasons.**
